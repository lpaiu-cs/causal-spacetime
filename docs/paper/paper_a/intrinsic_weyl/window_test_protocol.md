# Finite-N test of the order-only five-window correction

2026-11-21. **Frozen** in the commit that adds this file under the name
`window_test_protocol.md` together with the runner
`experiments/positive_control/intrinsic_weyl_window_test.py` and the checking
script `experiments/positive_control/check_intrinsic_weyl_window_test.py`.
That commit follows the engineering pilot of Sect. 8 and precedes every test
selection and measurement event. The draft this replaces
(`window_test_protocol_draft.md`, commits `46243eb` and `1e114c2`) left five
decisions open; the user took the proposed ones on 2026-11-21: tolerance
`t = 0.05`, `2^25` measurement events per window, 64 replicates, the
minimum-norm operator, the benchmark amplitude, independent windows.

## 1. Question

On the Ibrahim–Bombelli half-cycle wave the fifth-degree order-only inverse
reads the crest tensor about 12% low in the continuum, because the jet omits
the second covariant derivatives of the curvature
([sixth-degree record](second_derivative_results.md)). Two deterministic
continuum studies found a cheaper remedy than the sixth-degree jet:

- [Multi-window study](window_study_results.md): windows displaced along the
  observer's worldline (proper-time offsets 0, ±1/2, ±1 of the tip
  separation) measure the time-time second derivative; a five-point
  second-order correction of the centre window with a minimum-norm operator,
  which assumes no wave type, leaves 0.54% and 0.60% of the truth norm at 5.7
  to 6.1 times the events of one window.
- [Frame link](frame_link_results.md): windows that share the landmarks of
  their inner neighbour are aligned by a Procrustes rotation of the shared
  landmarks' inferred coordinates to 0.03°, and the corrected results equal
  those of oracle alignment, so the correction is order-only end to end.

Both were continuum calculations with nominal Gaussian errors. This test asks:
**given only the causal order of five windows along one observer's worldline
in the half-cycle wave, does the order-only five-window correction recover
the crest tensor's amplitude to within ±0.05, on the 95% two-sided interval
of its mean over independent replicates, while the single-window inverse on
the same data reads it low by more than 0.05?**

Scope, fixed now: the amplitude is the component of the estimate along the
true tensor (Sect. 4), one number. Full-tensor accuracy is reported
descriptively and is not claimed. One wave, one interval size, one amplitude.

## 2. Geometry, windows and truth

- **Wave.** The generator-side linearly polarized plane wave of the benchmark,
  `a(u) = A0 sin(pi u)`, `A0 = pi^2 h0 / 2`, `h0 = 0.01`.
- **Windows.** Index `w = 0, ..., 4` at proper-time offsets
  `delta_w = -1, -1/2, 0, 1/2, 1` of the tip separation along the geodesic
  `x = y = 0` of the Brinkmann chart; `w = 2` is the centre window. The window
  at `delta` is the standard window (tips `(0, 1/2, 0, 0)`, `(1, -1/2, 0, 0)`)
  of the profile `a(u + delta)`, generated by the window study's
  `build_window("sinusoid", A0, 2, delta)` (the Taylor series of
  `a(u + delta)` at `u = 0`, then `profile_plane_wave.ProfileWave`,
  unchanged). An event's chart coordinates in windows `delta1` and `delta2`
  differ by `(delta1 - delta2, delta2 - delta1, 0, 0)`.
- **Truth.** The crest tensor at the centre of the centre window (amplitude
  `A0`), expressed in the centre window's inferred frame by the O(3) alignment
  of its inferred landmark coordinates to their true normal coordinates,
  parity included (`aligned_tensor_error`, evaluation only).
- **Continuum expectations** (benchmark landmark layouts, cases 0/1, run
  covariances): single-window reading `0.8829 / 0.8880`; five-point second
  order with the minimum-norm operator `0.9959 / 0.9957`, with the plane-wave
  operator `0.9940 / 0.9944`; three-point first order `0.9697 / 0.9713`.

## 3. Procedure

Replicate `r` has seed `s_r`; window `w` draws its selection events from
`default_rng([s_r, w, 0])` and its measurement events from
`default_rng([s_r, w, 1])`. Windows are generated in the order 2, 1, 3, 0, 4.

1. **Selection events.** 4096 events of the window's wave in its own chart,
   between the two tips, labels permuted from the same stream (as in the
   benchmark generator).
2. **Candidates** (`w != 2`). The inner neighbour is the window one step
   toward the centre (2 for 1 and 3; 1 for 0; 3 for 4). Its own landmarks
   (Step 3, tips excluded) are moved into window `w`'s chart. Those with
   `0 < u < 1` that lie in the window's interval (after the lower tip and
   before the upper tip, decided by the generator) are included; the others
   are counted and dropped.
3. **Landmarks.** The extended selection order covers
   `[tip, own events, included candidates, tip]`. The own landmarks are the
   unchanged nested selection on the own events alone; the candidates do not
   enter it. A candidate is kept as a shared landmark if at least two own
   selection events lie between the lower tip and it, and at least two
   between it and the upper tip. Shared landmarks follow the own landmarks,
   before the upper tip.
4. **Clocks.** The landmark pairs with at least two own selection events
   between them; the tip-to-tip pair is not a clock. Every landmark keeps
   both tip clocks (true by Step 3).
5. **Archive.** Per window and level: the extended selection order, the
   design `nested`, the own count, the source row of every included candidate
   in the neighbour's landmark list, the landmark ids, the packed relations of
   the landmarks to the measurement events, and `N`. A worker re-derives the
   landmarks and clocks from the archive alone and refuses an archive whose
   saved landmarks differ.
6. **Measurement and fit.** `N` events; relation bits on the GPU, checked
   against the CPU path before data. Counts and the clock covariance as in
   `read_order_clocks`. The exact integer second moments come from the
   runner's `clock_second_moment`: the inverse's grouping of repeated
   membership patterns; memberships formed on the GPU from one chunk of 1024
   packed patterns at a time; every weight split into two parts of at most
   `2^13`, so that each FP32 product and partial sum is an integer of at most
   `2^23` (TF32 off); float64 accumulation across chunks. Its sums are the
   same integers as the inverse's float64 paths: checked before data, bit for
   bit, against the CPU and GPU float64 helpers on random patterns (weights up
   to `2.5e6`, more patterns than one chunk), and on a side-window archive of
   the first pilot attempt (Sect. 8). The fit is the unchanged fifth-degree
   inverse with curvature-square terms (`fit_joint`, model order 5,
   curvature-square correction, its four starts and optimizer settings). The
   pure fit is saved by a process that has imported no generator or truth
   module.
7. **Correction**, in a fresh process that loads only the five pure fits and
   the centre window's clock mask and covariance:
   1. Links: for `delta = ±1/2` the orthogonal Procrustes map, over O(3) (the
      SVD polar factor without a determinant fix), of the shared landmarks'
      inferred spatial coordinates onto those of the same events in the centre
      window; `±1` are chained through `±1/2`. The order does not fix an
      embedding's handedness, so two windows may have converged to mirror
      embeddings; the Weyl map of an improper link carries the parity that
      the tensor transformation gives.
   2. Each window's coefficients and nominal covariance are mapped into the
      centre window's inferred frame.
   3. Alias map: Weyl rows of `J5^+ Jd` at the centre window's inferred
      coordinates and zero curvature, whitened with its clock covariance
      (`alias_map`, `whitening`).
   4. Operator `B = minimum_norm_operator(alias . second_derivative_basis)`.
   5. Primary estimate: `correction_specs`' `five_point_second`, `h = 1/2`,
      `minimum_norm_time_time`, i.e. `sum_s W_s c_s` with
      `W_s = delta_s0 I - B d2_s / h^2 + B^2 d4_s / h^4`,
      `d2 = (-1, 16, -30, 16, -1) / 12`, `d4 = (1, -4, 6, -4, 1)`.
   The corrections are saved before any truth module is imported. Also saved,
   descriptive only: the single window; five-point second order with the
   plane-wave operator of the centre fit's principal null direction;
   three-point first order with the plane-wave and with the minimum-norm
   operator.
8. **Evaluation** (after Step 7 is saved): the truth of Sect. 2, the readings
   of Sect. 4, and descriptively the oracle relative frames (the normal frames
   of all windows are parallel transports of one another, so the oracle link
   is `R_s^T R_0` from the O(3) alignments `R`).

All fits and corrections use the `intrinsic_weyl_joint`, `intrinsic_weyl`,
`intrinsic_weyl_square` and `intrinsic_weyl_window_study` code as committed.

## 4. Statistics

For replicate `r` let `c_r` be the primary estimate and `u_r` the centre
window's estimate alone, both in the centre window's inferred frame, and `c`
the truth there. With the full-tidal inner product `<x, y>`:

- corrected reading `a_r = <c, c_r> / <c, c>`;
- single-window reading `s_r = <c, u_r> / <c, c>`.

Over the completed replicates, the means `a`, `s` and their 95% two-sided
Student-t intervals with `R - 1` degrees of freedom from the sample standard
deviations (house default coverage; the method is fixed now). The sample
standard deviation is used instead of the nominal covariance, so the
intervals do not rely on the calibration of the augmented windows.

## 5. Gates

- **G1, accuracy (primary).** ACCURATE if the interval of `a` lies inside
  `[0.95, 1.05]`; INACCURATE if it lies entirely below 0.95 or entirely above
  1.05; otherwise UNRESOLVED.
- **G2, control.** BIASED_LOW if the interval of `s` lies entirely below
  0.95; otherwise NOT_BIASED. If the single window is not biased beyond the
  tolerance, an ACCURATE G1 cannot be credited to the correction.
- **Primary claim.** INCOMPLETE if fewer than 58 of the 64 replicates (90%)
  complete; otherwise SUPPORTED if G1 is ACCURATE and G2 is BIASED_LOW;
  otherwise NOT_SUPPORTED. G1 and G2 are computed on the completed replicates
  and reported whatever the primary claim.

Strictness: an interval bound equal to 0.95 or 1.05 does not lie inside,
below or above it. House rule: G1 is an accuracy gate, decided on the 95%
two-sided interval and sized for 90% power in Sect. 7 with that same
construction. G2 is a sign gate.

A replicate fails if any of its five windows fails — selection (the nested
selection or the tip-clock requirement), a clock with zero measurement count,
a fit that the inverse's validity rule rejects (optimizer failure, rank
deficiency, boundary excess, non-positive full-tidal covariance) — or if a
link has fewer than eight shared landmarks, or the truth cannot be evaluated
for the centre window. A window that fails in generation stops its replicate.
Failures stay in the result and are never replaced: no redraw, reseeding,
sample extension, level change or tolerance change after outcomes. An
infrastructure interruption (a crashed process, a driver or allocation error,
a power loss; never an outcome) means incomplete. A rerun after an
engineering repair is allowed only for such an interruption, must be
disclosed with the original stop, and counts only if it reproduces every
replicate the stopped run completed (order archives, where kept, bit for bit;
selection records bit for bit; fits and corrections field for field).
Otherwise the stopped run's INCOMPLETE stands.

Descriptive, no verdict: the plane-wave and three-point readings; the mean
error vector in the generator frame and its full-tidal norm with a bootstrap
interval; electric and magnetic readings; per-window fits; shared counts, link
parities and Procrustes residuals; the angle between the order-only and the
oracle link, and the primary reading with oracle links (after the blind step).

## 6. Replicates, seeds, events, archives

`R = 64` replicates of five windows, `N = 2^25` measurement events per window,
seeds `s_r = 2026112600 + r`. Total `64 x 5 x 2^25 = 1.07e10` measurement
events and 1,310,720 selection events. Windows are independent sprinkles; in
one causal set they would share events where they overlap, which changes the
variance but not the expected reading, and the interval uses the replicates'
own spread.

Order archives are kept for replicates 0 and 31 and deleted after their fit
for the others, with the SHA-256 recorded by the fit; every window's
selection record (packed extended order, own count, candidate sources,
landmark ids), truth landmarks, pure fit and generation record are kept, and
the centre window's clock covariance is kept for replicates 0 and 31. At most
eight generated windows wait for their fits; three fits run in parallel.
Neither changes any number.

## 7. Power and cost (before data)

Inputs (`intrinsic_weyl_window_test_power.py`, commit `fd4e06e`, output
`window_test_power.json`): the one-window reading has standard deviation
0.113 at `2^24` (rms over 63 calibration selections x 100 orientations;
5-95%: 0.099-0.132); the corrected reading's variance is 1.29 times that
(minimum-norm, median over 400 orientations, larger of the two layouts), so
0.128 at `2^24` and 0.091 at `2^25`, assuming variance `~ 1/N`.

Replicates for 90% power of G1 at `t = 0.05`, minimum-norm operator:

| events per window | mean reading 0.996 | 0.986 | 0.976 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `2^24`, `kappa = 1` / 1.2 | 96 / 112 | 160 / 192 | over 256 |
| `2^25` | 48 / 56 | 80 / 96 | 160 / 160 |
| `2^26` | 28 / 32 | 40 / 48 | 80 / 80 |

`kappa` is a variance understatement factor; 0.986 and 0.976 allow a finite-N
bias of one and two percent below the continuum reading. At this design
(`2^25`, `R = 64`) the power of G1 is 0.98 (`kappa = 1`) and 0.94
(`kappa = 1.2`) at the continuum reading, 0.87 and 0.81 if the reading is one
percent lower, and 0.61 and 0.53 if two percent lower. A finite-N bias of two
percent would therefore most likely give UNRESOLVED, not a false ACCURATE.
The three-point first-order correction is not a candidate for the primary
gate: its continuum reading, 0.970, leaves a margin of 0.020 to `1 - t`, and
at `2^24` it does not reach 90% power within 256 replicates (0.77 at 256,
`kappa = 1`). G2 has power 0.985 at 64 replicates even with the one-window
variance at `2^24` and `kappa = 1.2` (single-window reading 0.885).

Cost, from the pilot (Sect. 8) instead of the design's model: at `2^24` a
window took 47-58 s to generate, and a fit worker 47-78 s (centre window) or
114-201 s (side windows, about 6,000 clocks against 3,700), of which 32-60 s
for the clocks. At `2^25` the run should be bound by generation, about six to
seven minutes per replicate, so about seven to eight hours in all, with three
fit workers keeping pace. Peak memory: under 3 GB per fit worker, 1.5 GB for
generation. The design's estimate of 16 GPU hours assumed the inverse's float64
GPU helper for the second moments; Step 6 removes most of that time.

## 8. Engineering pilot (before the freeze, not test data)

Two replicates with disjoint seeds (`2026112700`, `2026112701`) at `2^22` and
`2^24` events per window (nested prefixes of one sprinkle), engineering mode
of the uncommitted runner.

**First attempt** (`outputs/intrinsic_weyl_window_test_engineering_v1`,
stopped by hand after 13 minutes). The second moments then used the
inverse's GPU helper, which converts whole float64 membership blocks on the
host; its pinned staging memory grew to 35-44 GB per side-window fit at
`2^22` and 52-65 GB at `2^24` (the centre window, about 3,700 clocks, 6-16
GB), and with three fits in parallel the machine came within 12 GB of its
commit limit. It was stopped before any allocation failed; every fit that had
finished was valid. The bounded variant of Step 6 replaced the helper: on that
attempt's archive of window 1 of replicate 0 at `2^22` (6,029 clocks, 993,661
distinct patterns) it returns the identical matrix in 8.4 s and 1.0 GB,
against 56 s and 38.9 GB.

**Second attempt** (`outputs/intrinsic_weyl_window_test_engineering_v2`,
runner SHA-256 under the LF rule
`f890f9562f8c7e32c3aa3d82204b28e281879021b06b3e7589939c0fad092485`, the

complete, no failure, every fit valid.

- Shared landmarks per link 31-42. Procrustes relative residual 0.13-0.26%

## 9. Predictions (before data)

- `a` between 0.98 and 1.01; `s` between 0.86 and 0.90.
- Per-replicate standard deviation at `2^25`: corrected 0.08-0.10, single
  0.07-0.09.
- G1 ACCURATE, G2 BIASED_LOW, primary claim SUPPORTED.
- Shared landmarks 30-45 per link; links improper in about half of the
  replicates; Procrustes relative residual about `1e-3`.

## 10. Execution and provenance

1. This protocol, the runner and the checking script are committed together
   (the freeze). The runner's source is the one the pilot ran.
2. From the clean worktree at the freeze commit, the engineering pilot is
   replayed (`--engineering`) and compared with the pilot
   (`--compare`): selection records and order archives bit for bit, fits,
   corrections and evaluations within `1e-8`. Only if it reproduces does the
   test start.
3. The test runs once from the clean worktree (`--destination
   outputs/intrinsic_weyl_window_test_v1`), with no other GPU job. The runner
   refuses a dirty worktree or an existing destination, binds the hashes of
   its sources and of this protocol, and stops if any changes during the run.
4. After completion: the checking script (Sect. 11) with the refits
   `rep00:3:33554432` and `rep31:0:33554432`; then the summaries, the audit
   receipt and a results note are committed.

## 11. Checking script

`check_intrinsic_weyl_window_test.py` calls none of the runner's gate, link,
correction or evaluation code. It rebuilds the links by polar decomposition,
the Weyl maps of O(3) through the 3x3 electric and magnetic tensors (with the
pseudotensor sign of `B`), the five-point structure of the stencil weights
(recovering `B` from them), every corrected estimate, every reading, the
intervals and the verdicts. It re-derives every window's landmarks and shared
rows from its selection record with its own count rule. For replicates 0 and
31 it also re-derives the clock count, replays the first 4096 measurement
events of every window on the CPU, checks the order bits of the 300 pairs
nearest the flat cone and 300 random pairs per window with a general-purpose
ODE integrator, checks that no excluded candidate lies inside its window,
that shared landmarks are the moved landmarks of the neighbour and have the
same exact squared intervals in both charts (`1e-10`), recomputes the
minimum-norm operator, and recomputes the centre window's clock covariance
with the inverse's CPU float64 second moment (bit for bit against the saved
one, which came from the bounded GPU variant). It checks the truth's
plane-wave invariants and its
orientation in the generator frame, the blind boundary of every pure file,
and refits two windows in fresh processes (fields identical).

## 12. Scope

Whatever the verdict, this run establishes at most, for one plane wave at one
amplitude and interval size: that the order-only five-window correction
removes the fifth-degree bias of the amplitude along the true tensor at
finite `N` to within the tolerance, with the single window as control. It
does not establish full-tensor accuracy, the behaviour for other waves or
fields, the accuracy of one replicate (each replicate's own corrected reading
has a standard deviation of about 0.09, nearly twice the tolerance), or the
cost of a single-sprinkle implementation.
