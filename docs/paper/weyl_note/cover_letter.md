Dear Editors,

Please consider my manuscript, "Vacuum tidal curvature from causal order and interval counts: a finite-density estimator and a gravitational-wave benchmark", for publication as a research article.

**What is new.** Two preprints of October and November 2026 frame the question that the manuscript answers. Madsen (arXiv:2610.36695) proves that the Riemann tensor can be reconstructed from the causal order and element counts of a sprinkled causal set as the density diverges, and asks for numerical studies at finite density. Ibrahim and Bombelli (arXiv:2611.09056) estimate that detecting one Weyl component of a plane gravitational wave by discrete geodesic deviation requires between a hundred million and a billion elements, more than they can simulate. The manuscript presents an estimator of all ten vacuum Weyl components that uses order relations only, and reports what it achieved on sprinkled data in pre-specified tests: (i) tested coverage of its reported uncertainty, in 509 of 512 cases; (ii) errors of 3.5 to 7.4 per cent at dimensionless tidal norm 0.5; (iii) detection of the wave considered by Ibrahim and Bombelli with 17 million events, two to four orders of magnitude below their estimate for geodesic deviation; (iv) detection, with the estimator unchanged, of the static tidal field of a Schwarzschild exterior with 4.2 million events and, for an observer on a circular orbit, of its magnetic part; (v) calibration of the reported covariance on 64 flat replicates at up to 17 million events; and (vi) for the benchmark wave, a correction combining five windows along the observer's worldline that raised the mean amplitude along the true tensor over 64 replicates from 0.887 to 0.998 (95 per cent interval 0.977 to 1.018).

**Limits stated in the manuscript.** The tests cover vacuum plane waves and one Schwarzschild interval, seen by a static and by an orbiting observer. Detection is distinguished from accuracy: for the benchmark wave the local model underestimates the crest curvature by 12 per cent, the fit residuals of one window do not reveal this, and the manuscript traces the bias to omitted second derivatives of the curvature. In the geometry of the five-window test the side windows lie on nodes of the wave, so that test confirms mainly a bias operator inferred from the central window; it does not test the side windows or the links between windows, and it concerns the amplitude along the true tensor only. The manuscript claims no priority for curvature reconstruction from causal sets, no convergence theorem for the implemented procedure, and no efficiency advantage on equal inputs.
