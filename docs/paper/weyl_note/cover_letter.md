Dear Editors of General Relativity and Gravitation,

Please consider my manuscript, "Vacuum tidal curvature from causal order and interval counts: a finite-density estimator and a gravitational-wave benchmark", for publication as a Research Article.

**What is new.** Two preprints of October and November 2026 frame the question that the manuscript answers. Madsen (arXiv:2610.36695) proves that the Riemann tensor can be reconstructed from the causal order and element counts of a sprinkled causal set as the density diverges, and asks for numerical studies at finite density. Ibrahim and Bombelli (arXiv:2611.09056) estimate that detecting one Weyl component of a plane gravitational wave by discrete geodesic deviation requires between a hundred million and a billion elements, more than they can simulate. The manuscript presents an estimator of all ten vacuum Weyl components that uses order relations only, and reports what it achieved on sprinkled data: (i) tested coverage of its reported uncertainty, in 509 of 512 cases; (ii) errors of 3.5 to 7.4 per cent at dimensionless tidal norm 0.5 under a pre-specified accuracy criterion; and (iii) detection of the wave considered by Ibrahim and Bombelli with 17 million events, at a sprinkling density two to four orders of magnitude below their estimate for geodesic deviation.

**Limits stated in the manuscript.** The tests cover vacuum plane waves only. Detection is distinguished from accuracy: for the benchmark wave the local model underestimates the crest curvature by 12 per cent, and the manuscript shows that the fit residuals do not reveal this. It claims no priority for curvature reconstruction from causal sets, no convergence theorem, and no efficiency advantage on equal inputs.

It reports a different method and different results, all obtained after that decision, and shares no text, figures or data with the earlier manuscript.
